Featured Essay

The Quiet Architecture of Sovereign Debt: How Restructuring Shapes Development

July 2026  ·  12 min read

When a country can no longer service its debts, the process of restructuring that follows is rarely clean. It involves a tangle of creditors — multilateral institutions, bilateral lenders, private bondholders — each with different incentives, timelines, and leverage. Drawing on my research at AidData, I examine how the architecture of these negotiations shapes long-term development outcomes, and why the rise of non-Western creditors is rewriting the old playbook.

Who is Langston?

I'm a rising senior at the College of William & Mary, where I study Finance and Economics. I am a researcher, an essayist, and a musician. My work spans international development finance, statistical analysis, survey methodology, and creative expression.

My research at AidData has taken me across datasets from Ethiopia, Honduras, and Sub-Saharan Africa, with the goal of turning complex financial and geographic data into clear, actionable policy insight.

Outside of research, I serve as President of the William & Mary Symphony Orchestra, where I lead a 100+ member ensemble, direct finances, organize events, and am building toward our 2027 International Tour. I write about economics and markets on my Substack, Ceteris Paribus.

When I'm not working on a project, you'll find me on the tennis court, in the orchestra pit, or riding around Williamsburg on my bike.
